Section 10the question, exactly

Which questions earn the model

Most forecasts go wrong by answering a slightly different question from the one that will be scored. Here is the one being scored, word for word in substance:

Metaculus · question 45464 · 152 forecasters · community 25%

Resolves YES if any FEWS NET classification map with a "Reported" date after August 2026 and before January 2027 shows at least one area, urban settlement or displacement settlement in IPC Phase 5: Famine for the current period. Phase 4 areas marked "!" (would likely be Phase 5 without assistance) do not count.

The model only earns its place when a question has all four of the properties from section 01 plus a feedback loop. On 16 October five open Metaculus questions were forecast. Only one passed.

Section 11the timing curve

The answer is a date, not a yes or no

Take the 1,500 runs and ask, for each month ahead, what share have reached famine conditions by then. That cumulative curve is the model's whole forecast. Read it against the two deadlines from section 08.

Split by branch, the picture is starker than the average. In this model any one of the three shocks is enough to reach famine conditions within about four months, and no run without a new shock gets there at all. The 61% plateau is simply the chance that at least one shock hits. So the model's uncertainty is about whether a shock lands and how fast the chain runs, not about whether a shocked camp tips.

A limitation to say out loudA model where every shocked camp tips is too tidy. Real aid surges, migrations onward and harvest variation would let some shocked camps hold. That is a second reason the model enters the forecast as evidence at modest weight rather than as the answer.

The weakest link in that statement is the lags. They are judgement, not measurement. So the same 1,500-run experiment was repeated with every lag halved, cut by a quarter and stretched by half.

The model is confident about one thing and unsure about another. It is confident famine conditions will not form in the camps it models before December, even with every lag halved. It is unsure how soon after that they come.
Section 12from model to number

Why the model's near-zero is not the forecast

A model that says 0.1% should not be submitted as 0.1%. This one watches the assessed camps. It does not model besieged towns such as Kadugli and Dilling, where FEWS NET says some households already face Phase 5 and survey access is poor. That is the framework's "omitted node" risk, and it is exactly where a surprise classification would come from.

So the cascade enters as one piece of evidence among several. Each piece is a likelihood ratio: how much more likely this fact is in a world where the answer is YES. Ratios multiply the odds, which means they add on a log-odds scale. Starting from the base rate, the ledger below walks to the inside view.

Section 13the forecast

Meeting the crowd

The inside view does not stand alone. The Metaculus community is an aggregate of many forecasters with a good record, so it enters as a strong prior. The two are fused on the log-odds scale, each weighted by how much it is trusted: the community at 4, the inside view at 1.5. A confident inside view moves the result; it cannot take it over.

One decision still openThe pre-registered number applies a calibration slope of 0.9 after fusion, which pulls every answer toward 50%. Referenceglossary

Glossary

Activation gainA loop's strength at the moment it switches on. Above 1 with reinforcing sign means runaway.
Balancing loopA loop whose signs multiply to negative. It pulls a system back toward where it was.
Base rateHow often this kind of thing has happened before. The forecast's starting point.
Calibration slopeA post-fusion adjustment on the log-odds scale. Below 1 pulls toward 50%.
Dead-bandThe tolerance window inside which a domain passes on no influence.
Driver nodeAn external push that does not recover on its own. The right way to model a lasting shock.
FEWS NETFamine Early Warning Systems Network. Publishes the maps this question resolves on.
GAMGlobal acute malnutrition: the share of young children who are wasted. Famine threshold 30%.
IPCIntegrated Food Security Phase Classification, the five-phase scale from Minimal to Famine.
Likelihood ratioHow much more likely a fact is if the answer is YES than if it is NO.
Log-oddsThe scale on which independent evidence adds. Probability 50% is 0; 12% is about −2.
Monte CarloRunning the model many times with uncertain inputs drawn at random, and counting outcomes.
Reinforcing loopA loop whose signs multiply to positive. It makes a change bigger.
Set pointWhat counts as normal for a domain. In this framework it drifts with its surroundings.
